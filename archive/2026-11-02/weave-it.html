Watch fabric take shape.

Most fabric in 3D is a texture on a plane. Weave It builds the yarns, their crossings and their fibres. Version 1.3 adds new ways to build, animate and inspect that structure.

Grow — spread over a surface

Start at an origin and watch the cloth spread over an object. Tendrils reach ahead of a ragged edge, writhe, then settle onto the surface and knot into the fabric. Adjust Origin and Tendrils to shape the effect.

Cinematic Wrap — tendrils spread, settle and form the fabric.

Loom — watch every pick

Cloth flows from a weaving line at a steady speed. The warp opens into a shed, a shuttle carries each pick across from alternating sides, and the weft is beaten in. Add the optional satin-black Loom Head, adjust Head Size, or turn the head off to see the weaving itself.

Loom Head — a machine housing for the weaving animation.

Weave onto an object

Combine Loom with Wrap Onto Surface to weave over your target row by row. The machine head becomes a band that follows the surface, leaving fabric behind it as it moves.

Surface weaving — cloth builds directly over the target object.

Knit (Jersey) — real interlocking loops

One yarn per course loops through the next to form the familiar V stitch. Build knitted bands, sleeves and machine-knit looks, with plies, fibres, fuzz, colour, wrapping and Loom animation. Fringe and knots apply to woven patterns.

Knit Machine — interlocking loops with the Loom animation.

Fibers — get closer

Split every ply into fine fibres with loose flyaway loops for close-ups and macro shots. Ply twist and fibres stay attached to the yarn as the cloth moves; wrapped cloth rests above the surface, and fuzz is smoother.

From flat weave to 3D form

Wrap your weave onto shaped objects with UV-based, Spherical and Cylindrical wrapping. Follow a cushion, curved surface or rounded form while keeping the weaving controls editable.


From a flat weave to a 3D surface — animated with Wrap Morph.

Automatic surface-size matching helps adapt the overall weave dimensions to your target. For UV-based wrapping, use a suitable UV-unwrapped mesh; a continuous island with even scale gives the most consistent strand placement.

Surface wrapping follows your target geometry. It does not calculate cloth physics or collisions.

Control the character of every strand

  • Twisted yarns: use 1–8 plies per yarn and adjust twist amount, direction and flattening.
  • Round yarns or flat strips: change Profile Shape to move between thread, wire, ribbon and straw-like profiles.
  • Fibre-level detail: split plies into fine fibres with count, twist, thickness, flyaways and core controls. Use fuzz density, length, thickness and frizz for softer surface detail.
  • Frayed edges: use edge selection and Add Fringe, then tune length, variation, droop, unraveling and tip fibers.
  • Color, stripes and plaid: control warp and weft colors independently, add stripes in either direction and introduce per-strand variation.
  • Matching fiber color: fuzz follows its parent yarn, including its stripes and color variation.

Make the weave move

Use four built-in animation tools to build, wrap and animate your material. Weave Build now offers three styles: Grow, Loom and Reveal.

  • Weave Build — Grow spreads cloth from an origin with animated tendrils; Loom weaves with a shed, shuttle and optional machine head; Reveal keeps the original row-by-row animation.
  • Wrap Morph — Transition a flat weave onto an assigned surface.
  • Wind — Add movement to fringe and fibers.
  • Ply Spin — Rotate plies around the yarn.

Settings also support keyframes. Use the keyframe diamonds beside values, or record the setup with Keyframe All Settings.

A flexible Blender workflow

The procedural setup runs through a single Geometry Nodes modifier, with controls available in the Weave It panel. Add a preset, refine the weave and keep making changes throughout your project.

Simplify Viewport reduces editing detail while retaining full detail for rendering. For custom materials, shader attributes expose yarn color, random values, strand indices, fringe factors and yarn coordinates.

Requirements & working with detail

Blender 5.1 or newer. Tested in Blender 5.1.2, 5.2.0 LTS and 5.2.2 LTS.

Cycles recommended. Yarn geometry and hair-based fibers also support EEVEE.

Use the add-on’s preset creation tools to create the correct Hair Curves object for rendering fuzz and fringe fibers.

High strand counts, multiple plies and dense fibers increase geometry and render cost. Keep Simplify Viewport enabled while editing, and choose a detail level appropriate for your camera distance.

More control. Keep editing.

Tie real knots at every crossing or every Nth crossing for nets and macramé. Use Openness to space the yarns, add stripes or plaid from the panel, and change settings at any time in a fully procedural, non-destructive workflow.

Wrap by UVs on complex meshes, or use Spherical and Cylindrical projection for rounded forms without UVs. Yarns stop at UV seams instead of bridging through the object.

Updating from 1.2? Existing files keep working. Use Update Fabrics in the panel to upgrade them while keeping their settings. The original build animation remains available as Reveal.
