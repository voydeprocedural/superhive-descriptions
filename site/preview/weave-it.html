Weave It
Procedural Fabric & Weaving

Real fabric, from interlaced yarns down to individual fibers.

Weave It creates woven textiles, knitted fabric, reflective wire mesh and natural wicker with real geometry in Blender. Shape the yarns, wrap fabric onto an object, and animate it coming off a loom or growing over the surface.

Most fabric in 3D is a texture on a plane. Weave It builds the yarns, their crossings and their fibers, and version 1.3 adds new ways to build, animate and inspect that structure.

01 — NEW IN 1.3
Grow over a surface

Start at an origin and watch the cloth spread over an object. Tendrils reach ahead of a ragged edge, writhe, then settle onto the surface and knot into the fabric. Adjust Origin and Tendrils to shape the effect.

02 — NEW IN 1.3
Watch every pick on the Loom

Cloth flows from a weaving line at a steady speed. The warp opens into a shed, a shuttle carries each pick across from alternating sides, and the weft is beaten in. Add the optional satin-black Loom Head, adjust Head Size, or turn the head off to see the weaving itself.

Combine Loom with Wrap Onto Surface to weave over your target row by row. The machine head becomes a band that follows the surface, leaving fabric behind it as it moves.

Loom Head, a machine housing for the weaving animation.
Surface weaving, with cloth building directly over the target object.
03 — NEW IN 1.3
Knit (Jersey) with real interlocking loops

One yarn per course loops through the next to form the familiar V stitch. Build knitted bands, sleeves and machine-knit looks, with plies, fibers, fuzz, color, wrapping and Loom animation. Fringe and knots apply to woven patterns.

08 — WRAPPING
From flat weave to 3D form

Wrap your weave onto shaped objects with UV-based, Spherical and Cylindrical wrapping. Follow a cushion, curved surface or rounded form while keeping the weaving controls editable.

Automatic surface-size matching adapts the overall weave dimensions to your target. Wrap by UVs on complex meshes, where yarns stop at UV seams instead of bridging through the object, or use Spherical and Cylindrical projection for rounded forms without UVs. For UV-based wrapping, a continuous island with even scale gives the most consistent strand placement.

Surface wrapping follows your target geometry. It does not calculate cloth physics or collisions.

09 — STRAND CONTROLS
Control the character of every strand
◆Twisted yarns
Use 1-8 plies per yarn and adjust twist amount, direction and flattening.
◆Round yarns or flat strips
Change Profile Shape to move between thread, wire, ribbon and straw-like profiles.
◆Fiber-level detail
Split plies into fine fibers with count, twist, thickness, flyaway and core controls, and add fuzz with density, length, thickness and frizz.
◆Frayed edges
Use edge selection and Add Fringe, then tune length, variation, droop, unraveling and tip fibers.
◆Color, stripes and plaid
Control warp and weft colors independently, add stripes in either direction and introduce per-strand variation.
◆Matching fiber color
Fuzz follows its parent yarn, including its stripes and color variation.
◆Real knots
Tie knots at every crossing or every Nth crossing for nets and macramé.
◆Openness
Space the yarns apart.
10 — ANIMATION
Make the weave move

Four built-in animation tools build, wrap and animate your material.

Weave Build
Three styles. Grow spreads cloth from an origin with animated tendrils, Loom weaves with a shed, shuttle and optional machine head, and Reveal keeps the original row-by-row animation.
Wrap Morph
Transitions a flat weave onto an assigned surface.
Wind
Adds movement to fringe and fibers.
Ply Spin
Rotates plies around the yarn.
Keyframes
Settings support keyframes. Use the diamonds beside values, or record the setup with Keyframe All Settings.

12 — FAQ
Good to know
Updating from 1.2?
Existing files keep working. Use Update Fabrics in the panel to upgrade them while keeping their settings. The original build animation remains available as Reveal.
How do I render fuzz and fringe fibers?
Use the add-on's preset creation tools to create the correct Hair Curves object for rendering them.
How heavy is it to render?
High strand counts, multiple plies and dense fibers increase geometry and render cost. Keep Simplify Viewport enabled while editing, and choose a detail level appropriate for your camera distance.
