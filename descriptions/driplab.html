DripLab
Real Fluid Simulation & Painted Drips

Pour honey, chocolate, lotion or paint onto any product, painted in seconds or simulated for real.

DripLab puts liquids on your products in Blender, two ways. Paint them procedurally: strokes become drips, drizzles and glazes that follow the surface, and Pour drops a stream that pools and runs over the edges, live on the timeline with nothing to bake.

For hero shots, Simulate the pour for real. DripLab sets up Blender's own liquid solver around your product, with the viscosity and material of the preset: honey runs, lotion piles up, paint follows every curve of a face. Product Studio adds the backdrop, softbox lights and a depth-of-field camera in one click.

01 — KEY FEATURES
From a quick drip to a hero pour
◆Real simulation
Simulate Pour sets up Blender's liquid solver around the selected product, sized and aimed for you, with the liquid's real viscosity, worked out for the size the product has in life.
◆Shot Recipes
One button builds a Hero Pour, Speed Ramp Pour, Cream Swirl, Thin Drizzle or Pipette Drops around your product, with the studio and camera move that suit the shot.
◆Liquid Film
Closes the tears of thin liquid with no new bake, so stretched liquid stays one smooth surface.
◆Thicken After Pour
Creams and chocolate come to rest on their own instead of sliding off the product.
◆Pour, live
One click puts a source above the object; the procedural stream falls, pools and drips over the edges as you play, with nothing to bake.
◆Slow motion and close-ups
Slow Motion gives the liquid the look of a high-speed camera, and Close-Up spends the voxels where the camera is, for finer liquid at the same bake time.

Curved backdrop, softboxes, strip lights and a camera with depth of field, scaled to your product, in five styles.

The surface around the liquid turns darker and glossy, with an optional stain, without touching its material.
◆Paint with strokes

◆Toppings
Sprinkles, balls, flakes, bubbles, crumbs, drops and chips on top of the liquid.
◆43 presets

02 — SIMULATION
A real pour, set up in one click

Select your product and what it stands on, pick a preset and press Simulate Pour. DripLab builds the liquid domain around them, turns them into colliders and places a source you can move and tilt. Press Bake and Blender's own solver (Mantaflow) simulates the pour.

{"Every preset brings its physics. Water and serums run, honey and caramel flow slowly, lotions and creams pile up. Each liquid has its real viscosity, and Real Size tells DripLab how big the product is in life, so a 5 cm jar pours like a 5 cm jar however large you modelled it. The liquid gets the preset's material, toppings and a wet look on the product, and its surface is smoothed for close-ups. No extra software": 'the bake is a folder next to your .blend file, so it renders on any machine with Blender, render farms included.'}

03 — POUR
Select, pick a preset, press Pour

A source with an arrow appears above the object (or at the 3D cursor). Play the timeline and the stream leaves along the arrow, piles up a little where it lands, spreads into a pool that grows with the poured amount and runs over the edges in drips.

Pour Radius sets how far the pool spreads, Coat Drips how many drips run off, and Start Frame and Duration when and how long it pours. When the pour stops, the tail of the stream breaks off and falls.

05 — POUR AT AN ANGLE
Aim it like a real pour

Move the source to aim and rotate it to pour from the side. The stream leaves along the arrow and arcs down; Pour Speed sets how far the arc reaches.

06 — WETNESS
The surface soaks it up

Turn on Wetness and the surface around the fluid looks wet, darker and glossy, fading out over Wet Spread. It follows the fluid live, so a pour leaves a growing wet halo.

The surface object and its material are not changed. The wet look is a thin transparent layer that belongs to the fluid, so it works with any material in Cycles and EEVEE. Wet Stain lets the colour of the fluid soak in.

08 — INTERFACE
Everything in one sidebar tab

The DripLab tab in the 3D Viewport sidebar holds the preset gallery with previews, the Add Fluid and Pour buttons, Shot Recipes, the Simulation panel and Product Studio, and every setting of the selected fluid. The presets are also in the Asset Browser, ready to drag onto an object.

A bake is safe in the panel. Its physics lock once it is baked, since changing them would delete it; Free Bake asks first, then you change them and bake again.

10 — INSTALLATION
Up and running in a minute
1Download drip_lab-1.0.0.zip from your Superhive library. Do not unzip it.
2Drag the zip into Blender, or go to Edit ▸ Preferences ▸ Get Extensions, open the ⌄ menu at the top right and choose Install from Disk.
3Press N in the 3D Viewport and open the DripLab tab.
4Select an object, pick a preset and press Pour, then play the timeline. Or press Add Fluid and draw on the object.
11 — FAQ
Good to know
Is the simulation real?
Yes. It runs on Mantaflow, the liquid solver built into Blender, with nothing extra to install. DripLab sets it up for product shots; you bake it like any Blender simulation, and the bake is a folder next to your .blend file.
Can I change a simulation after baking it?
Yes: press Free Bake, change the settings or the preset, and bake again. Liquid Film and the toppings stay editable on a bake.
Do painted fluids need baking?
No. They are Geometry Nodes driven by the frame, so every frame can be scrubbed, re-timed and changed at any moment.
Cycles or EEVEE?
Both. The materials are procedural, and the wet layer uses blended transparency in EEVEE.
Is it heavy on the viewport?
Fluids are meshed from a volume. Lower Quality ▸ Resolution Viewport while you work and keep Use Render Resolution on for full detail in renders. Animated fluids update on every frame of playback, so hide the ones you are not working on or turn finished ones into meshes.
Does the file render without the add-on?
Yes. Fluids are Geometry Nodes and materials stored in the .blend file, so it also renders on a machine or render farm without DripLab.
Does it work on animated objects?
Yes: Stick to an animated object binds a fluid to a deforming mesh with Surface Deform or armature weights, and Unstick brings the editable fluid back.
Which Blender versions?
Blender 5.0 or newer. It was tested on Blender 5.0, 5.1 and 5.2.
Can I use it in commercial work?
Yes. Your renders and animations are yours to use in any project.
