Built to Stay Aligned

Move the light. Keep the pattern.
The gobo plane is parented to its Spot Light, so moving or rotating the light keeps the projection setup together.

Change the angle without rebuilding the setup.
The gobo plane automatically resizes when you change the spot angle, keeping the light cone covered.

Control the softness.
Use a smaller light radius for crisp shadows or a larger radius for softer transitions.

Keep the projection hardware out of the shot.
The gobo plane is hidden from the camera and reflections, while remaining part of the lighting setup.

Choose Your Workflow

From the GoboLab Panel

  1. Open the 3D Viewport sidebar with N and locate GoboLab.
  2. Click Load Gobo Light and choose a preset using the visual preview.
  3. Click Import Gobo Light to add the selected setup.
  4. Aim the light and adjust its settings to suit your scene.

From the Asset Browser

  1. Add the supplied library folder under Preferences > File Paths > Asset Libraries.
  2. Browse the rendered previews and drag a gobo into your scene.
  3. To edit the individual objects, disable Instance in the import panel that appears in the bottom-left corner.
  4. Position the light and adjust Power, Angle, and Radius.

Cycles & EEVEE

Black-and-white gobos
Project light patterns in both Cycles and EEVEE.

Colored stained glass
Full-color stained-glass projections require Cycles. In EEVEE, these presets project their pattern in grayscale.

Soft gradients, including clouds and caustics, may appear grainy in EEVEE at low sample counts. Try increasing Render Samples to 128–256 to improve the result.

A Few Lighting Ideas

  • Start with a radius of around 0.01–0.02 m for sharp window shadows, then adjust for your scene scale.
  • Try a warm light around 4500 K at a low angle for a sunlight-inspired look.
  • Combine different gobo lights to build a more layered composition.
